Your four questions

Can we control the status bar

Yes, three different levels of control, and they are worth separating because they behave differently.

LevelWhat it doesScope
Hide it in our launcherWindowInsetsController.hide(statusBars()) on API 30. Our home screen goes edge to edge.Only our screen. Netflix and the rest keep their own behaviour.
Draw our own stripHide the system bar, render a custom row with exactly the items you want, in our palette.Only our screen. This is what the mockups do implicitly.
Factory modeThere is a 12 Status Bar entry in the factory menu behind the factory-mode password.System wide, but vendor controlled and undocumented. Photograph before changing.

What we cannot do is restyle the system status bar's contents while leaving it in place. It is another app's window. We either live with it or replace it on our own screen.

Wallpaper

Supported, and cheaper than I told you earlier. Three options, in increasing cost:

  • Flat colour. Free, fastest, best at night. What A and B use.
  • Static image. About 3.5 MB resident. Load once with inPreferredConfig = RGB_565 and it halves to 1.8 MB, with no visible difference on a dark photo. This is what C shows.
  • System wallpaper. Read whatever the unit already has via WallpaperManager so it matches other screens. Same cost as static.

Live wallpapers are the one to avoid. They run a continuous render loop and that is exactly the drain this hardware cannot absorb.

Custom icon packs

Yes, the standard Nova and ADW pack format works and there are thousands of free ones. Mechanically: icon packs are ordinary APKs that expose a theme intent, hold drawables, and ship an appfilter.xml mapping each app's component name to a drawable name. The launcher queries installed packs, parses that XML from the pack's resources, and substitutes icons where a mapping exists, falling back to the app's own icon where it does not.

It is roughly two hundred lines and a settings screen to pick a pack. I would ship it in phase 2 rather than v1, because it is the kind of feature that is satisfying to build and easy to get wrong, and it changes nothing about whether the launcher is usable.
